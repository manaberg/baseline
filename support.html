01What Baseline needs

  • An iPhone with iOS 17.2 or later
  • An Apple Watch worn to sleep. Resting heart rate comes from any Apple Watch. Wrist temperature needs Apple Watch Series 8 or later, or any Apple Watch Ultra, and it only appears after about five nights of sleep tracking.

02No data showing up

  • Check that Baseline is allowed to read your data: Health app › your profile › Apps › Baseline. Turn on Resting Heart Rate and Wrist Temperature.
  • Baseline's Settings (the gear on the Today screen) lists which apps and devices each type of data comes from. If a type says "Nothing to read", Apple Health has no data of that type that Baseline is allowed to read.
  • Baseline needs about a week of nights to learn your baseline.
  • You can turn on Show sample data in Settings to see how the app works in the meantime. Sample data never mixes with your own data.

03What "off baseline" means

A night is off baseline when your resting heart rate or wrist temperature is clearly above your own recent average. Many things can do that: drinks, a late meal, a hard workout, stress, a cold or travel. Baseline shows the pattern. It doesn't say what caused a particular night.

If you were ill, press and hold that night in the Log and mark it as sick. Sick nights are left out of your baseline and your statistics.

Baseline is not a medical device and doesn't diagnose anything. If you're worried about your health, talk to a doctor.

04Cycle data

If you track your period in Apple Health, turn on Use cycle data in Settings. Baseline will then compare luteal-phase nights with your own luteal-phase baseline, so the normal rise in temperature after ovulation isn't counted as off baseline. Cycle data is off by default.
